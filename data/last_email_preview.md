## 1. Can Silent Retraction Fully Erase Traces of Chinese Misconduct Papers? An Investigation Into NSFC ‐Disclosed Academic Misconduct Cases

- 标题: Can Silent Retraction Fully Erase Traces of Chinese Misconduct Papers? An Investigation Into NSFC ‐Disclosed Academic Misconduct Cases
- 作者: Feifei Shen, Chenyang Wang
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Learned Publishing
- DOI: 10.1002/leap.2133
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7237118029
- 摘要: Given that the phenomenon of silent retraction seriously undermines the transparency and traceability of academic research, misleads follow‐up studies and weakens the effectiveness of the academic community's self‐correction mechanism and current attention to silent retraction of Chinese academic journal papers is insufficient, this study aims to understand the problem of silent retraction in Chinese academic journals and provide references for the high‐quality development of journals and the renewal of the academic environment. Based on the handling decisions of misconduct cases investigated by the National Natural Science Foundation of China from 2015 to 2024, this study statistically analysed the processing status and time of 46 Chinese scientific misconduct papers in databases, and investigated the current issue directories of 18 silently retracted papers and the handling policies of journals towards misconduct papers. The study found that silent retraction involved 18 papers from 17 journals; silent retractions cannot achieve the traceless effect claimed by third‐party intermediaries, as traces of the papers can still be retrieved across multiple academic platforms. Only six journals published academic misconduct handling policies on their official websites, and half of them were formulated after 2024. Accordingly, it is proposed that China should formulate recommended retraction standards to regulate metadata retention requirements drawing on existing international standards and best practices, journals need to establish clear retraction processes and Chinese journal databases should strictly define forms of paper retraction and deletion, reject unreasonable individual retraction applications and strengthen the inclusion and open access of retraction notices. Society at large should adopt a neutral stance towards retractions and needs to foster an environment treating retraction as a form of self‐correction instead of punishment for researchers.

## 2. Affiliation Matters! A Mixed‐Method Study of Barriers to Open Access Publishing for Researchers With Weak Institutional Ties

- 标题: Affiliation Matters! A Mixed‐Method Study of Barriers to Open Access Publishing for Researchers With Weak Institutional Ties
- 作者: Nataliia Kaliuzhna, Zeynep Aydin, Christian Hauschke
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Learned Publishing
- DOI: 10.1002/leap.2123
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7235903872
- 摘要: Rigorous, scrutinized and innovative research is taking place outside the conventional walls of academia. It is undertaken by independent scholars and researchers worldwide, including retired academics, citizen scientists, displaced researchers, medical patients, as well as scientists working for charities and civic organizations, independent consultants, and those employed in small enterprises. However, limited evidence exists on the challenges and barriers these researchers face in publishing open access (OA). To address this gap, this study employs a mixed‐methods exploratory design to examine the barriers encountered by researchers with weak institutional ties when attempting to publish OA. Our findings show that weakly affiliated researchers face a complex and interconnected set of barriers to OA publishing, including financial, practical, policy‐related barriers, as well as barriers shaped by prejudices. Using Miranda Fricker's concept of epistemic injustice, we show how these barriers not only make publishing more difficult but can also undermine researchers' credibility and limit their access to the knowledge and resources necessary for participating in scholarly communication. We conclude that, although various stakeholders can contribute to mitigating these obstacles, publishers in particular should adopt greater flexibility and recognize the diversity of contemporary scientific practice in order to support an epistemically just and inclusive publishing ecosystem. Responsibility should not rest on authors to conform to rigid and uniform publishing structures; rather, publishing systems must evolve to accommodate diverse research contexts.
