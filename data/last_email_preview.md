## 1. Enhancing or Transcending the Pipeline? How Incumbents Transition Toward Marketplace Platforms

- 标题: Enhancing or Transcending the Pipeline? How Incumbents Transition Toward Marketplace Platforms
- 作者: Alexander Engelmann, Georg Reischauer, Annabelle Gawer
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Journal of Product Innovation Management
- DOI: 10.1111/jpim.70063
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7225100025
- 摘要: ABSTRACT Business‐to‐business incumbents whose value creation rests on pipeline business models increasingly launch their own marketplace platforms. Yet how these business model transitions toward marketplace platforms unfold, and what they imply for the pipeline model, remains poorly understood. Adopting a cognitive perspective on managerial interpretations and decisions, we conduct a comparative case study of five marketplace platforms launched by incumbents in the European industrial products industry. We find that platform competition and the opportunities managers perceive in business‐to‐consumer platforms trigger platform creation, after which incumbents follow one of two pathways. In pipeline‐enhancing transitions, managers locate future value in the existing pipeline model and adopt platform features that strengthen it, resulting in cognitive alignment and interconnected platform–pipeline business models. In pipeline‐transcending transitions, managers locate future value in the marketplace platform and use business‐to‐consumer marketplaces as a reference point. This generates cognitive tensions with the pipeline model that ultimately resolve into independent pipeline and marketplace platform business models. Which pathway unfolds depends on the customer segments served, which shape managers' reference points for interpreting the evolving platform business model. Our process model explains these divergent outcomes and contributes to innovation management research on business model transitions toward platforms and their cognitive dynamics.

- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Journal of Scholarly Publishing
- DOI: 10.3138/jsp-2025-0129
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7225088702
- 摘要: A literature review on research publication in mainstream journals was carried out in this paper, focusing on global patterns, determinants, and strategies in contemporary scholarship (2015–2025). The authors synthesized evidence on cross-regional and cross-disciplinary differences in mainstream journal publication and examined personal, institutional, and systemic factors that influence publishing outcomes. Key factors influencing research publication in mainstream journals include collaboration, research self-efficacy, and persistence, while common barriers include limited and unevenly distributed funding, language restrictions, high publication costs, and weak research environments. Furthermore, strategies to strengthen publication outcomes at individual and institutional levels are discussed, with particular attention to settings where structural constraints limit access to global scholarly communication. The authors argue that improved access to reputable journals is essential for visibility, scholarly impact, and fairness in international academic publishing and call for research and policy interventions that address historically rooted inequalities rather than only their recent expressions.

## 3. The representation of SDG-related research in bibliometric databases: persisting imbalances and varying perspectives

- 标题: The representation of SDG-related research in bibliometric databases: persisting imbalances and varying perspectives
- 作者: Matteo Ottaviani, Stephan Stahlschmidt
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Scientometrics
- DOI: 10.1007/s11192-026-05857-x
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7116756232
- 摘要: Abstract Large bibliometric databases, such as Web of Science, Scopus, and OpenAlex, play a crucial role for decision-makers in science and science policy, as they are used as sources for informing decisions at both national and international levels, in public and private sectors. Although these databases facilitate bibliometric analyses, they are performative, affecting the visibility of scientific outputs and the measurement of participating entities. Recently, they have also incorporated the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) into their respective classifications, which have been criticized for their diverging nature. On another note, their infrastructural information processing is, of course, susceptible to emerging technologies. As a matter of fact, AI-supported and -powered tools have recently entered research practice and society at large. Large Language Models (LLMs), the branch of generative AI specifically focused on text, underlie their operation. By leveraging their features (i.e., in particular, mirroring what is thoroughly embedded in their training data under certain conditions), LLMs act as data magnifiers on SDG-classified publications to detect data biases that bibliometric databases are affected by. Within a broader perspective, our general setup serves as a conceptual exercise that characterizes the expected macro-level effects on the representation of SDG-related research in bibliometric databases, originating from the introduction of a generic LLM-based tool. Our analysis shows that the deployment of LLMs in the information processing of bibliometric databases reveals a systematic overlook in the data (i.e., scientific publications classified by SDGs) of the most disadvantaged categories of individuals, the poorest countries, and underrepresented topics that SDG targets explicitly focus on. Conversely, an unsolicited hegemonic role played by economic superpowers and Global North is identified.
