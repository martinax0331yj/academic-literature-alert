## 1. Public institutions, governance capability, and platform internationalization: institutional substitution at home and abroad

- 标题: Public institutions, governance capability, and platform internationalization: institutional substitution at home and abroad
- 作者: Nan Zhou
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Journal of International Business Studies
- DOI: 10.1057/s41267-026-00894-3
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7204280338
- 摘要: Abstract Digital platforms often expand internationally faster than public institutions can evolve, raising the question of how they sustain operations in weak institutional environments. I theorize governance capability, defined as the firm-level ability to design, implement, and enforce rules regulating participant behavior, as a dual institutional substitute. When home-country institutions are weak, platforms develop governance capability to fill domestic institutional voids (substitution at home), establishing order and trust where public enforcement is limited. Once developed, this capability becomes transferable across borders, enabling platforms to navigate weak host-country institutions during internationalization (substitution abroad). Its effect is conditional: it is stronger when home country institutions are stronger than those of the host country, and for broker platforms whose governance routines can be replicated globally at lower cost. Using a longitudinal sample of 50 digital platforms from China and the United States entering 61 host countries between 1996 and 2023, I find support for these predictions. These results position governance capability as a portable institutional infrastructure, offering a capability-based alternative to structural responses such as diversification in institutionally weak contexts, and highlighting its role as a supply-side driver of platform internationalization that complements demand-side forces such as network effects.

- 期刊或来源: New Media & Society
- DOI: 10.1177/14614448261476588
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7204248329
- 摘要: This article examines the contemporary digital audiobook through the lens of platformization and develops a conceptual framework for understanding platformized audiobook culture. Drawing on an integrative review of interdisciplinary scholarship, supplemented by market reports, industry statistics, and audience studies, it uses the German audiobook market as an analytically illuminating example through which broader processes of platformization can be explored. The article situates audiobooks within wider transformations of everyday digital media practices, highlighting the shift from physical formats toward downloads and subscription-based streaming services. Conceptually, it distinguishes platformized audiobook culture from digital audiobook culture through two complementary analytical perspectives adapted from book studies, digital publishing, and media and communication studies: an integrative platform model and a data-environment model of streaming services. Building on this synthesis, the article proposes an ecosystem model of platformized audiobook culture to support future theoretical and empirical research.

## 3. Government institutions and data custodianship over personal information: Comparing citizen preferences in Hong Kong and South Korea

- 标题: Government institutions and data custodianship over personal information: Comparing citizen preferences in Hong Kong and South Korea
- 作者: Jung Eun Kim, Kwan Nok Chan, Wai Fung Lam, Edward Kei Fung Chan
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Government Information Quarterly
- DOI: 10.1016/j.giq.2026.102169
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7204168251
- 摘要: Public administration researchers traditionally approach the use of personal information in public service through the lens of direct user control, but a combination of cognitive constraints and rapid digitization has made privacy-preserving choices exceedingly difficult for citizens to manage independently. Recent research has shifted toward the notion of “data custodianship”, where public institutions serve as custodians of personal information. In a survey experiment conducted in Hong Kong ( n = 1031) and South Korea ( n = 1040), citizens evaluated their trust in various institutions serving as custodians tasked with managing biometric information for facial recognition. The findings suggest that while institutional trust can be influenced by accountability mechanisms, it is also shaped by individuals' experiences with public institutions, as well as their political orientation, social trust, and privacy preferences. Trust in one functional domain of data custodianship—such as data collection—may not carry over to others, like storage or management. Custodianship is only viable as a policy alternative to user control in data-intensive solutions if its design accounts for variation in public trust across societies, individuals, and aspects of data governance.
