## 1. Effectiveness evaluation of generative intelligent search: a comparative study with traditional search engine

- 标题: Effectiveness evaluation of generative intelligent search: a comparative study with traditional search engine
- 作者: Fan Li, Chunxiu Qin, Xubu Ma, Yulong Wang, Ruijia Zhang
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Aslib Journal of Information Management
- DOI: 10.1108/ajim-01-2026-0032
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7225750099
- 摘要: Purpose The emergence of generative artificial intelligence has transformed information retrieval, yet limited understanding of its differences from traditional search engines hinders clarity regarding changes in user search behavior and variations in search effectiveness, thereby impeding guidance for future search interface design. Design/methodology/approach We conducted a laboratory experiment comparing two versions of the Bing search engine: the traditional Bing search and the generative-AI integrated Bing (Bing Chat). We interpreted variations in user search behavior through querying, browsing, and clicking data, and established a multidimensional metric framework encompassing user, cost-benefit, and system dimensions to assess search effectiveness. 40 participants with good search skills completed the experiment, resulting in a final dataset comprising 160 observation records. Findings The Mann-Whitney U test revealed that users of generative AI search engines exhibited the following characteristics compared to those using traditional search engines: longer queries, more keyboard activity, more frequent query restructuring (including more narrowing queries, more following the system queries, and more changing the syntactic structure queries, with less broadening queries), more browsing during the first search session, and less scrolling and clicking. The results of the Linear Mixed Models show that, compared to traditional search engines, users achieve superior performance with generative AI search on users’ subjective perceptions and search efficiency metrics. Furthermore, we employed machine learning to validate which search behaviors can predict a user’s search effectiveness. The results indicate that the Gradient Boosting Machines are the optimal prediction models. The five most significant indicators for predicting search performance are Avelength. QueryConsTime, TimetofirSearch, ResViewTime and NumbofScroll. Research limitations/implications By revealing differences in user behavior and search efficiency across various search engines, this study deepens our understanding of generative AI search engines at a finer granularity and contributes to building adaptive search platforms capable of optimizing search recommendations. Originality/value By revealing differences in user behavior and efficiency between traditional and generative versions of the Bing search engine, this study deepens our understanding of generative AI search engines at a finer granularity and contributes to building adaptive search platforms capable of optimizing search recommendations.

## 2. Citation Verification as a Standard Component of Peer Review: Reflections and Recommendations in the Era of AI-Generated Scholarship

- 标题: Citation Verification as a Standard Component of Peer Review: Reflections and Recommendations in the Era of AI-Generated Scholarship
- 作者: Mary-Louise E. Millett
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Journal of Scholarly Publishing
- DOI: 10.3138/jsp-2026-0041
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7225752324
- 摘要: Peer review is essential to maintaining the integrity of the scholarly record, yet reference lists often receive limited scrutiny. As generative artificial intelligence (AI) becomes increasingly integrated into scientific writing, this oversight poses growing risks. Large language models can generate citations that appear legitimate but are partially or entirely fabricated, allowing unverifiable references to enter published literature. Such citations may evade plagiarism detection software and standard editorial safeguards. Drawing on one reviewer’s experience and literature on peer review and research integrity, this article argues that fabricated references constitute falsification, undermiing reproducibility, distorting evidence hierarchies, and compromising scientific integrity. It highlights gaps in existing peer-review guidelines, particularly the absence of explicit expectations and tools for citation verification, and proposes practical, low-burden recommendations for reviewers and journals. Integrating routine citation spot-checking into peer review represents a feasible, scalable approach to preventing AI-generated citation fabrication and strengthening accountability in scientific publishing.
