## 1. Impact of Data Privacy Regulations on Recommender Systems Performance

- 标题: Impact of Data Privacy Regulations on Recommender Systems Performance
- 作者: Liben Chen, Meizi Zhou, Yicheng Song, Gediminas Adomavičius
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Information Systems Research
- DOI: 10.1287/isre.2024.1230
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7224887821
- 摘要: Data protection regulations like GDPR and CCPA let consumers delete personal data or opt out of collection, but companies struggle to understand how these practices affect their recommendation systems. This matters because recommendations drive substantial revenue; according to industry reports, Netflix’s system saves more than $1 billion annually, and personalized suggestions generate 35% of Amazon purchases. We analyzed how different data privacy practices impact recommendation accuracy using large-scale simulations with real-world data. A critical finding: Context matters enormously. For services where user preferences are more stable (e.g., streaming platforms), opting out barely affects recommendation quality while deleting data cuts performance dramatically. However, for platforms where preferences and needs can change frequently (e.g., e-commerce), deleting old data can actually improve recommendations while opting out severely harms them. For policymakers, this means one-size-fits-all regulations may be counterproductive. For companies, we demonstrate that data privacy practices can be redesigned: users can protect their most revealing data while maintaining close-to-optimal recommendation quality. This challenges the tradeoff between privacy and personalization, showing both could be achievable through smarter design. Companies should offer context-appropriate privacy options; regulators should recognize different platforms need different approaches.

## 2. Opulent and Environmentally Responsible?: Brand‐ and Consumer‐Related Factors Shaping Consumer Responses to Sustainable Product Innovation Signals by Luxury Brands

- 标题: Opulent and Environmentally Responsible?: Brand‐ and Consumer‐Related Factors Shaping Consumer Responses to Sustainable Product Innovation Signals by Luxury Brands
- 作者: İsmail Erzurumlu, Mehmet Berk Talay
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Journal of Product Innovation Management
- DOI: 10.1111/jpim.70065
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7236644127
- 摘要: Academic Summary Luxury and environmental sustainability often appear fundamentally at odds: luxury derives its value from exclusivity, symbolic aspiration, and resource‐intensive craftsmanship, while sustainability demands restraint, reduced environmental impact, and responsible production. Yet luxury brands such as LVMH and Gucci are increasingly introducing sustainable product innovations, seeking to align with evolving consumer expectations and regulatory demands. For consumers, this creates a genuine tension: does a sustainably produced luxury personal good elevate its desirability, or quietly erode what makes it special in the first place? Despite the urgency of this question for both consumers and brands navigating the luxury‐sustainability interface, research on consumer responses to sustainable product innovation in luxury personal goods remains limited. Drawing on signaling and cue‐diagnosticity theory, this study examines how, why, and when consumers respond to luxury brands'' environmentally sustainable product innovation. Across a pilot and three main experimental studies, it explores consumer responses to sustainability signals, particularly considering skepticism surrounding the sustainability–luxury interface. Results show that consumers have more positive attitudes, higher purchase intentions, and greater willingness to recommend luxury brands engaged in sustainable versus conventional product innovation. This effect is partially mediated by consumers' feelings of specialness, a proximal, state‐like psychological mechanism. Moreover, transparency as a brand‐level signal and socially responsible consumer behavior as a consumer‐level characteristic strengthen the effectiveness of sustainable product innovation signals. These findings advance understanding of how environmentally sustainable product innovation creates value in luxury markets by shaping consumers' interpretations, experiences, and responses. Managerial Summary Luxury brands face growing pressure to integrate sustainability into their products while preserving the exclusivity, prestige, and craftsmanship that define their appeal. This research offers practical guidance on how they can navigate this challenge through sustainable product innovation. Across four studies involving more than 2450 consumers, introducing eco‐innovative products improves consumer attitudes, increases purchase intentions, and strengthens willingness to recommend. A key insight is that consumers respond positively because sustainable product innovations make them feel special, unique, and privileged, reinforcing rather than undermining luxury's core appeal. Managers can cultivate these perceptions by embedding environmental sustainability into limited editions, exclusive collaborations, and bespoke offerings. Experiential storytelling highlighting material origins, craftsmanship processes, and environmental significance transforms these initiatives into emotionally engaging experiences rather than purely ethical propositions. Transparency is equally critical. When brands are transparent about their sustainability practices, the positive impact on consumer attitudes and purchase intentions is significantly stronger. These results offer clear guidance for luxury brand managers and innovation teams: integrate sustainable product innovation into new product development not as a trade‐off but as a source of differentiation, prioritize transparency to meet consumer demand for accountability, and tailor communications to eco‐conscious consumer segments, positioning the brand as a forward‐thinking leader in luxury innovation.
