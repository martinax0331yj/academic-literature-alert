## 1. Social media presence and engagement performance of University Libraries in Türkiye

- 标题: Social media presence and engagement performance of University Libraries in Türkiye
- 作者: Ali Kavak
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Journal of Librarianship and Information Science
- DOI: 10.1177/09610006261465926
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7171951993
- 摘要: Social media have become central to how academic libraries communicate with users, yet systematic evidence on their use in Türkiye remains limited. This study provides a comparative, census-based analysis of the social media presence and engagement performance of university libraries in Türkiye. A complete enumeration of 207 university libraries (129 state, 78 foundation) was examined across five platforms Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, and YouTube. Data on platform presence, follower counts, posting frequency, and a follower-to-enrollment ratio were collected manually from official websites and accounts and analyzed descriptively. Instagram was the most widely adopted platform (64.7%), followed by Facebook (46.9%), X (39.1%), YouTube (26.1%), and LinkedIn (10.6%). Foundation university libraries achieved higher enrollment-normalized reach, particularly on Instagram and LinkedIn, whereas several large state university libraries combined high absolute follower counts with low per-student reach. Content production varied markedly, with intensely active accounts coexisting with many dormant ones. The findings indicate that, although presence has grown over the past decade, opening an account differs sharply from managing it as a sustained, purposeful communication channel. By foregrounding the follower-to-enrollment ratio as a normalized indicator, the study offers one of the first comprehensive, comparative assessments of academic-library social media in Türkiye.

- 标题: Forecasting Zombies: Analysis of AI Predictive Capacity of Genre-Specific Content Trends for Books
- 作者: Laetitia Cassells, Rory Cassells
- 年份: 2026
- 期刊或来源: Publishing Research Quarterly
- DOI: 10.1007/s12109-026-10070-y
- URL: https://openalex.org/W7168134942
- 摘要: Abstract While there is an increasing awareness and application of artificial intelligence (AI) tools in the book publishing industry globally, it is often restricted to analysis of quantitative data and information, such as those shared by data aggregation subscription databases. This analysis is completed in a constantly evolving genre and technological tool developmental environment, where the proliferation of non-specialist tools and “scaffolded” user interactions with large language models (LLMs) are the norm. Considering the high cost of quantitative tools and the nature of machine and deep learning predictions, this paper explores the use of other AI tools for qualitative analysis, such as natural language processing and LLMs, drawing on practices reported by publishers themselves. The use of these tools to facilitate commissioning and acquisition decisions in publishing enterprises is challenged when considering the veracity and validity of these types of recommendations, as well as the lack of transparency regarding the function of these types of AI analysis tools. Using an extensive bibliography of genre-specific titles in the subject of Zombie Horror this paper presents an analysis of recommendations forecasting trends in the book market and explores the factors influencing the success of recommendations using publicly available AI tools.
